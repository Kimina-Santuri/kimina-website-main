Five Beats: Drums, Bass, Chords, Song

What makes a beat sound like house, hip-hop, Afrobeats, amapiano or gengetone?

Today you run the session yourselves, in pairs. First you'll build five drum patterns. Then you'll add chords and a bassline to your favourite one and arrange it into a short song. Each mission has something to hear on this page, a game, and a box to tick. Total time is about 3 hours.

Download the printable PDF

Pair rules

  • Take turns. One of you works in Ableton while the other reads the grid out loud and checks each note. Then swap and build the same thing on the other set.
  • Listen first. Press Play on the page before you build, so you know what you're aiming for.
  • Kick first, then snare or clap, then hats. Build one row at a time and listen after each.
  • Mistakes are fine. If it sounds wrong, your partner compares the clip to the grid, note by note.

Set Up the Drum MachineOne bar, sixteen steps.

  1. Open Ableton Live and start a new set. Make a MIDI track.
  2. In the Browser, go to Drums and drag a kit (a 909 or 808 kit is perfect) onto the MIDI track. That's your Drum Rack.
  3. Double-click an empty clip slot to make a 1-bar clip. Double-click the clip to open the note editor.
  4. Right-click the note editor and set the grid to Fixed Grid → 1/16. Now you see 16 boxes per bar.
  5. Press B to switch to Draw mode. Click to add a note, click again to remove it.

How to count one bar

The bar has 4 beats. Each beat is split into 4 steps: "1 e & a, 2 e & a…". The dark boxes are the beats. Every grid on this page uses step numbers 1–16.

Game: Count Along

Turn Live's metronome on and press play. Together, say "1 e & a 2 e & a 3 e & a 4 e & a" out loud in time. Then one of you calls out a random step number ("step 7!") and the other points to it on the screen.

HouseSteady, danceable, kick on every beat.

124 BPM909 kitFour-on-the-floor
  1. Set the tempo to 124.
  2. Kick on every beat: steps 1, 5, 9, 13.
  3. Clap on beats 2 and 4: steps 5 and 13.
  4. Closed hat on every "&": steps 3, 7, 11, 15. This offbeat hat is what makes house bounce.

Game: What's Missing?

One of you closes your eyes. The other mutes one row (select its notes and press 0). Guess which drum went missing. Swap and repeat three times.

Hip-Hop (Boom Bap)Slower, heavy snare, a little swing.

90 BPM808 or acoustic kitSwing
  1. Make a new clip and set the tempo to 90.
  2. Kick on steps 1, 8 and 11. That's the "boom".
  3. Snare on steps 5 and 13. That's the "bap".
  4. Closed hat on every other step: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15.
  5. Swing it: open the Groove Pool (the wavy-lines button at the bottom left of the Browser), pick a 16th swing groove such as MPC 16 Swing-58, and drag it onto your clip.

Game: Beatbox It First

Before building, one of you beatboxes the pattern: "boom … tss … BAP …". Then build it and compare. Was the beatbox right? Then toggle the groove on and off: can you both hear the swing?

AfrobeatsBouncy kick, clap on 2 and 4, busy shaker.

104 BPMKit with shaker and rimBounce
  1. Make a new clip and set the tempo to 104.
  2. Kick on steps 1, 4, 9 and 12. The early kicks on 4 and 12 give it the bounce.
  3. Clap on steps 5 and 13.
  4. Rim on steps 7 and 15.
  5. Shaker on all 16 steps. Then make the in-between shakers quieter: select a note and drag its velocity bar down in the lane under the notes. The grey boxes on the grid are the quieter ones.

Game: Move One Kick

One of you moves one kick note one step left or right. The other listens and votes: still Afrobeats, or broken? Try three different moves. Which kick matters most?

AmapianoFour-on-the-floor kick, rolling shaker, and the log drum.

113 BPMAny kit + a low tomLog drum
  1. Make a new clip and set the tempo to 113.
  2. Kick on steps 1, 5, 9 and 13, like house, but keep it softer.
  3. Clap on steps 5 and 13.
  4. Shaker on all 16 steps, louder on 3, 7, 11 and 15.
  5. Log drum on steps 4, 7, 11 and 14. Use the lowest tom in your kit for now. It's the deep, bouncy sound amapiano is known for, and in Part 2 it becomes your bassline.

Game: Kick or No Kick?

Amapiano tracks often drop the kick out for whole sections. One of you mutes the kick for 4 bars, then brings it back. The other says when it feels better: with or without? Try the same trick on the house beat. Does it work as well?

GengetoneNairobi's street sound: hard kick and a dancehall snare.

100 BPM808 or punchy kitDembow snare
  1. Make a new clip and set the tempo to 100.
  2. Kick on every beat: steps 1, 5, 9, 13. Make it loud and punchy.
  3. Snare on steps 4, 7, 12 and 15. This is the dancehall "dembow" rhythm that gengetone is built on.
  4. Closed hat on every other step: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15.

Game: Desk Drummers

Play the beat. One of you taps the kick on the desk while the other claps only the snare. Then mute the snare in Live and keep clapping it from memory for 4 bars. Unmute: are you still in time? Swap parts.

Chords

  1. Make a new MIDI track and drag on a keys or pad sound from Instruments (any piano or pad preset is fine).
  2. Make a 4-bar clip. In the clip's Notes panel, set the scale to A Minor and switch Scale on. The in-key rows light up, so you can't hit a wrong note.
  3. Draw one chord per bar, each note lasting the whole bar:
    BarChordNotes
    1A minorA3 · C4 · E4
    2F majorF3 · A3 · C4
    3C majorC4 · E4 · G4
    4G majorG3 · B3 · D4

Bassline

  1. Make another MIDI track with a bass sound (search the Browser for "bass"). For amapiano, use your log drum tom instead.
  2. Make a 4-bar clip. The bass plays the root of each chord: A in bar 1, F in bar 2, C in bar 3, G in bar 4. Use the octave around A1–A2.
  3. Put the bass notes on the steps shown in the "Bass" row above. It changes with each genre: house bass sits on the offbeats, hip-hop and Afrobeats bass follows the kick.

Game: Who Left the Band?

Use the Drums, Bass and Chords switches above (or mute tracks in Live). One of you closes your eyes, the other turns one layer off. Name the missing layer. Then try a mood swap: play the chords in the order F – G – Am – Am. Happier or sadder? Keep the order you like.

Simple ArrangementTurn a 4-bar loop into a 32-bar song.

4 sections8 bars eachAdd, then take away
  1. Make a copy of your drum clip and call it Drums – no kick. Delete the kick notes in the copy.
  2. Press Tab to switch to Arrangement View.
  3. Drag your clips onto the timeline following the chart. Select a clip and press Cmd+D (Mac) or Ctrl+D (Windows) to repeat it until it fills 8 bars.
  4. Intro (bars 1–8): hats or shaker and chords only.
  5. Build (bars 9–16): add the bass and the clap or snare. Still no kick.
  6. Drop (bars 17–24): everything, with the full drum clip and kick.
  7. Outro (bars 25–32): take the kick and snare out again, so it's chords, bass and hats.

Bonus: in the last bar of the Build, delete all the drums for one bar. The silence makes the Drop hit harder.

Game: Where Are We?

One of you clicks somewhere random on the timeline and presses play while the other looks away. The listener names the section (Intro, Build, Drop or Outro) by ear. Three in a row and you swap.

Listening PartyPlay your song to another pair.

  1. Swap seats with another pair.
  2. Play each song from the start. Listeners don't look at the screen.

Game: Name That Genre

For each song, the listeners guess the genre and say which drum gave it away. One point per correct genre, one bonus point for naming the right drum. Then they say the exact moment the Drop started. One point if they're right to the bar.
